Life After Laparoscopic Surgery: Recovery Tips

By Dr. Vamseedhar Konati • General & Laparoscopic Surgeon, Tirupati

Laparoscopic (keyhole) surgery uses small incisions and specialized instruments, which generally means less pain and a faster return to normal life compared to traditional open surgery. Here's what recovery usually looks like and how to make it smoother.

The First 24-48 Hours

Mild soreness around the small incision sites, some shoulder-tip discomfort (from gas used during surgery), and tiredness are common. Most patients are encouraged to start gentle walking within a day to aid circulation and reduce gas-related discomfort.

Diet and Hydration

Start with light, easily digestible meals and plenty of fluids. Your surgical team will guide you on when to resume a normal diet based on the specific procedure performed.

Caring for Your Incisions

Keep the small incision sites clean and dry as instructed. Watch for signs of infection such as increasing redness, swelling, warmth, or discharge, and report these promptly.

Activity and Rest

Gentle movement helps recovery, but avoid heavy lifting or strenuous exercise until your surgeon confirms it's safe — usually a few weeks, depending on the procedure.

When to Call Your Surgeon

  • Fever or chills
  • Increasing pain not controlled by prescribed medication
  • Redness, swelling, or discharge at incision sites
  • Persistent nausea or vomiting

Most patients return to normal daily activities within a week or two, though this varies by procedure and individual health. Always follow your surgeon's personalized recovery plan.
