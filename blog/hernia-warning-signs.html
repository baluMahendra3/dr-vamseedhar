5 Warning Signs of a Hernia You Shouldn't Ignore

By Dr. Vamseedhar Konati • General & Laparoscopic Surgeon, Tirupati

A hernia occurs when an internal organ or tissue pushes through a weak spot in the surrounding muscle wall, most commonly in the abdomen or groin. Many people ignore the early signs until the condition becomes painful or complicated. Here are five symptoms to watch for.

1. A Visible Bulge

A soft lump or bulge under the skin — often in the groin, navel, or near a previous surgical scar — that becomes more noticeable when standing, coughing, or straining is one of the most common early signs.

2. Discomfort That Worsens With Activity

A dull ache or pressure that increases with lifting, bending, or prolonged standing, and eases when lying down, can indicate a developing hernia.

3. A Feeling of Heaviness or Pulling

Some people describe a dragging or heavy sensation in the abdomen or groin, especially by the end of the day.

4. Pain While Coughing, Sneezing, or Straining

Sudden, sharp discomfort during activities that increase abdominal pressure is a common early warning sign.

5. Nausea, Vomiting, or a Bulge That Won't Go Back In

This can indicate a strangulated or incarcerated hernia, which is a medical emergency requiring immediate surgical attention. Do not delay seeking care if this happens.

When to See a Surgeon

Hernias do not resolve on their own and typically require surgical repair, often possible through minimally invasive laparoscopic techniques with faster recovery times. If you notice any of the signs above, it's best to get evaluated early rather than wait for complications.
